Ninety days sounds like a lot until you divide it across 19 subjects. The students who use that time well don't try to revise everything equally. They decide **what to revise first**, then make sure they come back to it.

This plan splits your last three months into three phases of 30 days each.

## Days 1–30: Cover the most-tested topics

Start with the topics that appear most often in past NEET-PG, INI-CET and FMGE papers. These carry the most weight for the time you spend on them.

- Pick the top-priority topics in every subject, not just your favourite ones.
- Aim for confident recall, not perfect notes.
- Finish each day with 20–30 past questions on what you just revised.

> Past questions help set revision priority. They don't predict the next paper or replace full-syllabus study.

## Days 31–60: Widen your coverage

Once the high-yield core is in place, move down the priority list. This is where most of your new topics will come from.

1. Work subject by subject, in milestone-sized chunks.
2. Revisit phase-one topics once a week so they don't fade.
3. Track coverage so you can see which subjects are falling behind.

## Days 61–90: Revise, test, repeat

The final month is for consolidation. Learn as little new material as you can.

- Take full-length mock tests at least twice a week.
- Review every mistake and link it back to its topic.
- Do one last quick pass over the highest-priority topics in the final 10 days.

## Keep it sustainable

A plan only works if you can follow it for 90 days. Protect your sleep, take one lighter day a week, and adjust the plan when life gets in the way. Steady progress beats a perfect timetable you abandon in week three.
